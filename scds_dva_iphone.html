SCDS DVA — legibility under probe

Why this exists. Repeating the same fixed sound many dozens of times in one session risks the same thing your own voice does versus a recording of it: after enough exposures, your system may learn "this exact sound predicts that exact symptom" and quietly discount it — which would make an effect shrink or vanish on a rerun for reasons that have nothing to do with the sound itself.

This tool replaces the subjective severity rating with a brief, mostly-involuntary task: a 1 second probe with a short random number flashed in the middle of it, and you report whether you could read it — plus, optionally, what you actually saw, so the reading can be scored right or wrong rather than trusted at your word. That is much harder to consciously discount than a symptom rating, and it stays a fair comparison even if compensation is doing something real underneath.

Three modes. Fixed list repeats a small set of conditions many times — same structure as before, but now the report breaks results down by which repetition this was, so a habituation effect would show up directly as a trend rather than being argued about after the fact. Random sweep draws a fresh, never-repeated frequency (plus random drift and side) every trial, so there is essentially nothing for a stimulus-specific prediction to lock onto. Aggressive random goes further: a fresh random-width frequency band plus a randomly chosen sampling function (sweep, triangle, quadratic, random jumps, or several simultaneous tones) every trial — the report renders that one as a frequency × band-width heat-map.

Mode

Frequency list

Random sweep range

20 Hz–20 kHz spans three orders of magnitude. Sampled linearly, almost every trial would land above 1 kHz and the low end — where every previous session's effects have clustered — would barely get tested. Log-uniform gives the low, middle and high end roughly equal coverage.

Aggressive random

Each trial draws a random frequency band instead of a single tone: a width from a log-uniform draw over the range below, then a centre frequency drawn log-uniformly so the band always stays inside the outer bound — then one of five sampling functions moves through that band for the probe's full duration.

Sampling function per trial, equally likely: linear sweep (straight ramp, random direction), triangle (sweeps to the other end of the band and back within the same trial), quadratic (an accelerating or decelerating sweep instead of a constant rate), random jumps (independent random frequency every ~30–70 ms within the band — noise-like rather than a glide), or multi-tone (2–4 simultaneous static tones at independent random frequencies in the band, each scaled down so the sum can't clip). All still respect the same per-frequency level caps as the other modes, using the most restrictive point in the band.

Digit task

The flash sits centred in the probe window — with the defaults, that's 250 ms of probe, then a 500 ms flash, then 250 ms more of probe.

Random silence-except-sea before each probe, so the exact onset time is never predictable. This is the only thing about a trial's timing that's ever randomised on screen.

Probe level

Caps: below 300 Hz, up to 0 dBFS. 300 Hz–6 kHz (the most sensitive region, and where the acoustic-trauma history matters most), capped at −18 dBFS. Above 6 kHz, capped at −10 dBFS.

Sea ambient

High-passed at 180 Hz, so it has no energy anywhere near the low frequencies where past sessions found the most effect, but real energy from ~300 Hz up.

Font size check

A random number flashes on repeat, at the exact duration used in the real trials. Adjust the size down until it's the smallest you can still read reliably while everything looks stable — that's the legibility threshold this session's probe trials get compared against, so it's worth getting a real answer rather than a comfortable one.
