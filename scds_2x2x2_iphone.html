SCDS 2×2×2 — frequency × drift × side

Eight conditions, fully crossed and equally sized: 20 Hz vs 500 Hz × steady vs aggressively drifting × left vs right ear, plus silent sham trials scattered through. Nothing on screen ever indicates which condition a trial is — not during it, not after it.

The hypothesis this is built to test: if 500 Hz reaches the labyrinth mainly by air conduction it should be strongly ear-specific, while if 20 Hz arrives substantially by bone conduction it should reach both sides regardless of which channel is driven. That predicts a laterality effect at 500 Hz and none at 20 Hz — an interaction, not a main effect.

Two things are measured on every trial and both bear on that: your symptom rating, and your guess at which ear was driven. The guess is not a nuisance check here — it is a primary outcome. If you can name the side at 500 Hz and genuinely cannot at 20 Hz, that is the bone-conduction prediction showing up directly, independent of any symptom report.

Silent sham trials are scattered through and watched as the session runs, not just scored afterwards: one symptomatic sham quietly schedules another silent trial within the next few trials to re-check, and two symptomatic shams in a row stops the session and asks whether to continue — at that point the background rate has likely risen for the whole session, not just for one unlucky trial, and everything measured past it would be unreadable.

Probe level, set separately per frequency

Earbuds are vastly more efficient at 500 Hz than at 20 Hz, so the same digital level is nowhere near the same sound pressure. There is no way to fix that without a calibrated microphone. What you can do is match them by ear, which makes the cross-frequency comparison interpretable-ish and the within-frequency comparisons (drift, side) exactly as clean either way.

Sea ambient

Off by default, and the reason matters for this particular test. The sea recording is high-passed at 180 Hz, so it has real energy at 500 Hz and none anywhere near 20 Hz. It would therefore mask one arm of the frequency comparison and not the other — and worse, it would mask the arm where you expect the laterality effect to live, which is the main thing this session is for. Two previous sessions showed the sea has no measurable effect on outcome, so turning it on costs little; but it is not free here the way it was there.
