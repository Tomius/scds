SCDS Threshold Staircase — iPhone

Measures how loud the probe has to be before you lose full compensation, separately for each ear, instead of hammering both ears at maximum and recording a category. The level is the measurement here, so most trials sit near your threshold rather than at the ceiling — that makes the session both gentler and more sensitive than the fixed-level design.

Two staircases (left ear, right ear) run interleaved in random order. Answer "can't fully compensate" and that ear's next tone gets quieter; answer anything else and it gets louder. Each ear converges on its own 50% point, and the difference between the two is a direct, numeric lateralisation measure in dB.

The session opens with three silent trials and will tell you if it shouldn't proceed. If symptoms are already present with nothing playing, a staircase can only track that background — so it's better to find out in the first minute than after the whole session. More silent trials are mixed in blind later to check the baseline hasn't drifted.

The washout between trials is self-paced: you decide when you're back to baseline, and the next probe won't start until you say so. How long that takes is recorded, since recovery time is worth having and a fixed timer was throwing it away.
⚠ Read before proceeding

This delivers low-frequency audio at full system volume that may provoke oscillopsia, imbalance, nausea, or vertigo. Do not proceed if standing, near fall hazards, driving, or in an active severe vestibular episode.

Stop immediately with the red STOP button on pain, sharp tinnitus increase, or disabling vertigo. Do not drive for at least 30 minutes afterwards.
